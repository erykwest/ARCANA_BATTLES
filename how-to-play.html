Field guide

How to play

COFFEE BATTLES is a real-time tactical sandbox. You deploy a force, give orders to regiments and try to collapse the opposing army by combining movement, facing, energy, morale and terrain.

1. Set up the battle

Start from the battle setup screen, choose the available points and the battle configuration, then compose and deploy your force. Units begin inside their deployment area and can be positioned before the battle starts. The setup is intentionally quick so a new test can begin without a long campaign layer.

2. Select and inspect units

Use the left mouse button to select a unit. Drag a selection box to select several units, and use Ctrl while selecting to build a multi-unit selection. The side interface exposes the current unit state and the available commands. Use the mouse wheel to zoom and right-drag to pan across the field.

3. Move with purpose

A regiment has a front and takes time to turn. Movement is therefore not only about reaching a coordinate: approach angle affects how quickly a unit can bring its front toward an enemy and whether it risks exposing a flank or rear. Multiple waypoints can be used to shape a route, while the pathfinder attempts to avoid impassable obstacles and other units.

4. Manage formations

Infantry-sized units can change formation. The standard formation is a balanced block, while line and column arrangements trade frontage against depth and manoeuvrability. Formation changes take time, so changing shape during contact is very different from preparing before the engagement.

5. Choose a stance

Stance changes how autonomous behaviour is handled:

  • Hold keeps a unit conservative and prevents it from seeking unnecessary contact.
  • Aggressive encourages a unit to close and engage when a valid enemy is nearby.
  • Elusive favours disengagement and distance rather than commitment to melee.

Routed troops effectively prioritise escape rather than normal combat behaviour.

6. Charge and retreat

A charge is a short burst of commitment rather than ordinary movement. Cavalry gains the most from a clean approach and has the speed to exploit exposed flanks and rears. A badly aligned charge, a blocked route or contact against the enemy front can waste that advantage. Retreat orders deliberately break contact and are useful when a unit needs to recover before morale collapses.

7. Use archers as ranged support

Archers attack at range and can acquire targets without having to enter melee. If a selected target is outside shooting distance, they can move toward effective range before firing. Ranged fire does not consume the same combat energy as melee, but archers remain vulnerable if enemy units reach them. Aggressive behaviour is what allows them to commit more readily to close combat; firing itself is a separate action.

8. Watch energy and morale

Movement and melee consume energy. A tired unit loses combat effectiveness, so constant movement can arrive at the decisive fight already exhausted. Morale is a separate pressure: casualties, prolonged combat and exhaustion can push a unit toward breaking. Once morale collapses, the unit flees and attempts to recover rather than continuing a normal fight.

9. Read the terrain

The field can include water, walls, hills, forests and roads. Water blocks normal movement, walls can provide cover, hills influence line of sight and ranged reach, forests obstruct sight and slow movement, and roads reward movement along useful routes. Cavalry is especially sensitive to terrain that constrains open manoeuvre.

10. Win by breaking the opposing force

The victory bar at the top of the battlefield tracks the relative collapse of the two armies as units are eliminated or forced out of effective combat. The aim is not simply to click the closest target, but to create favourable contacts while preserving enough energy and morale to continue fighting.

Prototype note: balance values and AI behaviour are still being tuned. If a unit behaves unexpectedly, a short description and reproducible steps are useful feedback.
