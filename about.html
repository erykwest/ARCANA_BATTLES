About the project

A tactical sandbox built around battlefield behaviour

COFFEE BATTLES is an independent browser game prototype focused on the behaviour of battlefield units rather than on collection systems, progression trees or long pre-game setup.

What the project explores

The core question is simple: how much tactical depth can emerge from a compact set of readable rules? Regiments move as formations, have a meaningful front, flank and rear, consume energy while moving and fighting, and can lose morale under pressure. A charge is not just a movement speed bonus; its direction, timing and point of contact matter. Ranged troops need distance and line of sight, while terrain can change both movement and engagement choices.

The game is therefore designed as both a playable battle and a systems laboratory. A short battle can expose whether pursuit is too sticky, whether a pathfinder chooses poor routes around an obstacle, whether cavalry has enough room to exploit mobility, or whether morale breaks too early. Iteration is deliberately fast: the project can be changed, deployed and tested without a large build pipeline.

Current scope

The public build concentrates on field battles. The current system includes deployment, infantry, cavalry, archers, formations, stances, charges, retreats, ranged fire, energy, morale, terrain, pathfinding and a victory state. The battlefield is intentionally bounded and legible so that the effect of each rule can be observed.

COFFEE BATTLES is still in active development. That means balance, interface details and unit behaviour may change between versions. The public site documents the current intent of the project rather than promising a fixed final feature set.

Design principles

  • Readable cause and effect. Players should be able to understand why a unit slowed, broke, charged or failed to reach a target.
  • Low friction. A battle should begin quickly and restarting a test should be easy.
  • No mandatory account layer. The current game can be opened and played without registration.
  • Systems before decoration. Movement, combat and AI behaviour are prioritised before large content libraries or presentation-heavy features.
  • Non-interruptive monetisation. The design goal is to keep the battle itself free from disruptive advertising placements.

Why “COFFEE BATTLES”?

The name reflects the intended scale of the experience: something you can open, understand and play in a short session, while still giving the underlying simulation enough depth to reward repeated experimentation.

Development status: public prototype. The current build is playable, but systems and balance remain subject to change.
